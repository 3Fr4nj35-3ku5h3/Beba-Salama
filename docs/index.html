05 How It Works

Built on data.
Honest about its limits.

Understanding what Beba Salama does — and what it cannot do — is as important as the findings. Transparency is not optional when the subject is human safety.

📡
Two Data Sources, Combined

Layer 1 — World Bank/Ma3Route: 31,064 geolocated crash reports, Aug 2012–Jul 2023. Every crash has GPS coordinates, timestamp, and flags for fatalities, matatus, boda bodas, and pedestrians.

Layer 2 — Kenya Police/HDX: 1,118 ground-level police records from 2016–2017 with road names, cause codes, and county breakdowns.

🗺️
Blackspot Zone Computation

25 blackspot zones were computed using DBSCAN spatial clustering (ε=300m, min 8 crashes) on all 31,064 records. Each zone carries a real crash count, fatality rate, peak danger hour, and a composite risk score. These are the zones the Drive Mode monitors in real time.

⚖️
The Severity Score

No single column captures crash severity, so a composite score is engineered:

Severity =
+5 × fatality flag
+3 × pedestrian involved
+2 × matatu involved
+2 × motorcycle involved
+2 × independent report count
Score ≥ 5 → classified HIGH RISK · 16.8% of all crashes
🚗
Drive Mode — How It Works

Drive Mode uses navigator.geolocation.watchPosition() for continuous GPS tracking. Haversine distance is calculated from your position to every blackspot zone centre every few seconds. On entry: full-screen alert, phone vibration (400ms × 3), and Web Speech API audio warning in Kenyan English. The alert does not re-trigger for the same zone until you exit and re-enter it.

Best on a phone with location on. On desktop it still opens the map and zone layers — GPS alerts require a device that can report position.

Features — time + location only

LightGBM classifier trained in src/train_model.py, evaluated with 80/20 stratified split. Leaky inputs (n_crash_reports, matatu/motorcycle text flags) were removed after proof: 1,456/1,456 crashes with 3+ reports and zero other flags were automatically high-risk by construction of the target. Honest AUC is 0.61 — not the inflated ~0.81 leaky version. Feature importance: latitude/longitude, then hour. The Route Risk checker on this page is a browser distill of that model (corridor + hour tables) plus a small mode overlay. Full investigation: notebooks/03_model_training.ipynb.

⚠ What this platform does not know
  • Crowdsourced bias: Layer 1 depends on who tweets. Urban, smartphone-connected reporters are over-represented. Rural routes are likely under-counted — not lower-risk.
  • Historical data only: All crash records end July 2023. This is a risk intelligence tool, not a live traffic system. New road conditions, construction, or safety improvements after that date are not reflected.
  • Engineered severity, not ground truth: The high-risk label is a composite score (fatality, pedestrian, matatu, motorcycle, report count) — not an official police severity class.
  • Distill ≠ full booster in the browser: The Risk Checker uses model-derived hour and corridor probabilities (same feature philosophy as the AUC 0.61 model). It is not a live LightGBM binary running in JavaScript; mode is a labelled vulnerability overlay only.
  • GPS accuracy varies: Drive Mode proximity alerts depend on device GPS accuracy, which can be ±50m indoors or in dense urban areas. On desktop without location, use Demo drive.
  • Consequences of being wrong: A corridor flagged high-risk affects real operators and passengers. Any production deployment needs confidence intervals, a feedback loop, and clear ownership when predictions fail.
  • Accessibility gap: A risk score only helps people with a phone and data. The platform should be judged on whether it eventually reaches pedestrians and boda riders who carry the highest risk — not only smartphone-owning matatu passengers.
06 Key Findings

Six things the data reveals
about Kenya's roads.

Not assumptions. Each finding is derived directly from 31,064 crash records spanning eleven years.

01
Peak severity: 05:00Counter-intuitive
5am is the deadliest hour — not midnight

5am registers the highest average crash severity of any hour — when long-distance trucks, early matatus, and boda bodas converge on empty roads with maximally fatigued drivers and near-zero emergency response capacity.

02
Night: 11.0% fatalDay: 6.9% fatal
A night crash is 60% more likely to kill you

Lower visibility, higher speeds on quieter roads, delayed emergency response, and driver fatigue compound into a 60% elevation in mortality probability. Every journey between 9pm and 4am carries this invisible premium.

03
Sunday: highest fatality rateFriday: highest volume
Sunday kills more. Friday crashes more. Two different problems.

Friday carries the highest raw crash volume. Sunday's lower volume conceals a dramatically higher fatality rate, driven by long-distance inter-county journeys, alcohol involvement, and reduced medical access in rural areas.

04
2,541 crashes8.2% of incidents
Matatus carry a disproportionate share of Kenya's road death

Public service vehicles appear in 8.2% of all recorded crash events. Given 14 seats per matatu, each incident carries a fundamentally different human cost than a private vehicle crash.

05
Peak: 6,101 in 2015Recent: 1,805 in 2022
Crash volume has fallen since 2015 — but the work is far from done

A meaningful downward trend reflects road safety interventions and enforcement campaigns. However, 1,805 recorded incidents in 2022 in a dataset that captures only a fraction of total crashes means progress and crisis coexist.

06
5,206 high-risk events16.8% of crashes
One in six crashes shows detectable pre-journey risk signals

16.8% of all crashes carry a high-severity classification. All inputs — time, location, vehicle type, day — are knowable before a journey begins. Risk is not uniformly distributed. Some combinations carry measurably higher historical crash severity. AUC 0.61 on held-out data confirms meaningful signal, not perfect prediction.
